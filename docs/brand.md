# Holidaze — Brand Statement

> A booking site that reads like a magazine.

## Target audience

- **Primary — the design-literate traveller.** 28–45. Reads _Cereal_, _Monocle_, _Kinfolk_, _Wallpaper\*_. Travels three to six times a year, often off-season, often by train. Picks a place for the paragraph it's described in, not the price band. Books direct when the option exists. Keeps a guidebook on the shelf after the trip is over.
- **Secondary — the independent host.** Owns or runs one to three properties: a restored farmhouse, a design apartment, a boutique B&B. Writes their own descriptions. Resents being flattened into a platform's template, where a ten-bedroom chain hotel and their grandmother's cottage are rendered by the same card.
- **Geography.** Europe-first: the atlas, the gazetteer, and the city set all lean that way. English-reading, with a preference for European cities and mid-sized towns over capitals.

## Three words

**Curatorial. Hand-set. Unhurried.**

- _Curatorial_ — nothing's on the page by accident. The atlas isn't a widget, it's a choice of which twelve cities represent the map this week. Even the 404 has a point of view.
- _Hand-set_ — the page behaves like a letterpress proof, not a dashboard. Numbered sections, hairline rules, drop-caps, mono captions carrying ticket numbers. No round corners. No soft shadows.
- _Unhurried_ — one hero reveal on load, then stillness. No scroll-jacking, no parallax, no micro-delight twenty seconds in. Reduced-motion isn't a fallback here, it's the baseline; motion is opt-in.

## Design direction

Four visual moves carry the brand. They are the shape of the thing — the _how it looks_ before any specific font or hex gets argued about.

- **Magazine-spread, not product-page.** The venue detail is a spread: cover image up top, italic masthead, drop-cap opening paragraph, marginalia running down the outside edge (specs, amenities, address, host). Listings elsewhere borrow from magazine contents pages: numbered, eyebrow-labelled, sectional. A user should feel they're turning pages, not scrolling a feed.

- **Atlas-like.** Travel lives in printed atlases before it lives in map tiles. Holidaze takes the atlas metaphor seriously: a typographic world plate with italic city names, a saffron gazetteer, equirectangular graticule, coordinates rendered in mono. Maps at Holidaze are _read_, not dragged.

- **Bento grid.** The home page and search results use a modular grid of cells; each an interactive surface that lifts when engaged and compresses its neighbours into a muted background. The grid is allowed to break: a cell can span two columns, an editorial block can straddle three. The bento is the site's spatial signature.

- **Generous whitespace.** One idea per view. Wide margins. Air between sections sized to a printed page, not a wireframe. If a designer feels the urge to add one more element, the answer is almost always to remove two. The page is a composition; the whitespace is the composition's rhythm.

These four hold together because they share a posture: the site is a printed object, rendered in a browser. Every other decision: the type, the palette, the motion; exists to serve that posture.

## Feels like

- A **Wallpaper\* City Guide** — typographic, photograph-forward, the kind of book travellers keep on the shelf.
- A **Cereal** travel issue — generous whitespace, italic captions, one photograph allowed to carry a whole page.
- A **Monocle _Escapist_** annual — numbered sections, pigmented accents used sharply, confidence without swagger.
- A **Thames & Hudson** atlas — india-ink coastlines, saffron gazetteer type, lapis graticule.
- An **Aesop** store window — not because of the product, because of the restraint.

**Does not feel like:**

- Airbnb. Booking.com. Trivago. Agoda.
- A SaaS dashboard with pastel gradients and a geometric sans.
- Anything with a chatbot bubble bottom-right.

## Accessibility is a brand value

The palette, type and scrims are graded against **WCAG AA** — not as a compliance chore, as a brand commitment. Body copy sits comfortably above the contrast floor, photograph overlays are deepened until card text still reads against the darkest portion of any hero image, and the hot accent is restricted to display sizes and italic emphasis nested inside ink paragraphs.

## Point of difference

A booking site that reads like a magazine. That's the whole pitch. Every component is built to protect that sentence:

- The venue page is a **spread**, not a listing.
- The receipt is a **document**, not a modal.
- The atlas is a **plate**, not a map tile.
- The search bar is a **sentence you finish**, not a form you fill in.
- The command bar is for readers who'd rather **type than click**.

If a future feature can't defend itself against _"does this make Holidaze read more like a magazine?"_  then it belongs on a different product.
